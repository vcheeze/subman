# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Individuals auditing their personal subscriptions — not power accountants or household budget managers. They open Subman when a renewal is coming up, when they notice another charge on their card, or when they simply want to know what they're paying for and whether it's still worth it. Context is reflective, not frantic: a quick clarity check, not a guilt trip.

## Product Purpose

Subman helps people understand the true value of their subscriptions. The goal is not to minimize cost at all costs — it's to maximize value. Success looks like users staying subscribed to what they genuinely use and enjoy, with full awareness of what each subscription costs over time and how much value they're getting back. The app makes recurring spend legible so decisions feel informed, not impulsive or shame-driven.

## Brand Personality

Witty and playful, with Monzo/Revolut energy: approachable about money, friendly in tone, but never flippant about the numbers. Humor lives in copy, micro-interactions, and occasional surprises — not in cluttering the UI. Three words: **clear, candid, cheerful**. The product should feel like a sharp friend who helps you see what you're actually paying for, not a lecture or a spreadsheet.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages: purple gradients, hero metrics ("$X saved!"), identical icon + heading + blurb feature grids
- Typical budgeting apps: guilt-tripping copy, alarmist spending alerts, spreadsheet-first layouts that make subscriptions feel like failures
- Corporate fintech: navy-and-gold palettes, stock photography, trust-badge walls, stiff institutional tone
- AI-default UI patterns: cream/sand body backgrounds, eyebrow kickers on every section, side-stripe card accents, gradient text

## Design Principles

1. **Clarity over guilt** — Surface true cost and value without shaming what users choose to keep.
2. **Playful honesty** — Wit in copy and moments (Monzo-style), with real respect for the money underneath.
3. **Awareness before action** — Informed decisions beat impulsive cancellations; the app earns trust by showing, not nagging.
4. **Respect the subscription** — Each sub is a choice worth understanding, not an error to eliminate.
5. **Show, don't spreadsheet** — Make value and spend visible at a glance; depth when asked, never table-first by default.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA as baseline. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for all animations. Ensure body and placeholder text meet contrast requirements against tinted backgrounds — especially muted foreground on near-white surfaces.
